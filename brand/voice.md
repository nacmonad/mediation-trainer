# Obversa Brand Voice

**Status:** Approved foundation for closed alpha  
**Date:** 2026-08-30

## 01. Voice overview

Obversa speaks with the composure of an experienced practitioner who understands that difficult conversations resist scripts. It is precise about process, candid about limitations, and respectful of the mediator's judgment. The language is human without becoming casual, and serious without sounding legalistic or clinical. It explains the product plainly, then lets concrete mechanics carry the argument.

**Voice essence:** Obversa sounds like a trusted practitioner beside you: calm, exact, and perceptive.

## 02. Tone dimensions

### Human, with professional restraint

**Casual ←──●──→ Formal**

Use natural language and contractions where they help. Never become chatty, flippant, ceremonial, or bureaucratic.

> Practice the difficult mediation before it begins.

### Calm, not inert

**Measured ←─●───→ Energetic**

Prefer composed statements and useful verbs. Create momentum through clarity, not exclamation marks or manufactured urgency.

> Choose a Scenario, meet the Parties, and begin the Session.

### Serious, with quiet wit

**Serious ←─●───→ Playful**

The subject deserves respect. Occasional understated wit is welcome in low-risk contexts, but never around confidentiality, failure, evaluation, or user mistakes.

> Two Parties should be more than one chatbot wearing two name tags.

### Expert, but accessible

**Accessible ←──●──→ Authoritative**

Use mediation terminology when it is the clearest language for practitioners. Explain product mechanics without inflated technical detail or claims of superior wisdom.

> Each Party sees only the Events it is authorized to observe.

### Warm, not intimate

**Distant ←───●─→ Intimate**

Show respect for the work and the person doing it. Do not presume emotions, manufacture belonging, or imitate therapy language.

> Your judgment stays in the chair.

## 03. Voice qualities

### Composed

Obversa remains steady around conflict, uncertainty, and product limitations. It does not sensationalize difficult Sessions.

- **Do:** “An impasse is a possible outcome, not a product failure.”
- **Don't:** “Crush conflict and close more agreements.”

### Exact

Name the actor, action, boundary, and current capability. Prefer a specific product fact over a broad assurance.

- **Do:** “Caucus Events are visible only to the Mediator and the participating Party.”
- **Don't:** “Your conversations are protected by advanced privacy.”

### Practitioner-respecting

Obversa supports professional judgment rather than claiming to replace or grade it. Address mediators as capable peers.

- **Do:** “Review what happened, then decide what you would carry into another Session.”
- **Don't:** “Let AI tell you the right way to mediate.”

### Perceptive

The voice recognizes that Parties can hold different facts, meanings, and constraints at once. It makes complexity legible without collapsing it into false certainty.

- **Do:** “The same exchange can look different from each side of the room.”
- **Don't:** “Discover the objective truth behind every conflict.”

### Candid

State what is simulated, planned, uncertain, or prohibited. Trust grows from visible limits, not blanket reassurance.

- **Do:** “The closed alpha uses synthetic Scenarios. Do not enter real case material.”
- **Don't:** “Practice safely with complete peace of mind.”

### Useful

Every sentence should orient, explain, or help someone act. Remove throat-clearing, trend language, and decorative profundity.

- **Do:** “Request an invitation to test the closed alpha.”
- **Don't:** “Step into the future of transformative dispute resolution.”

### Product language

- Use **mediator** or **practitioner**, not “learner,” unless discussing an educational cohort.
- Use **Party** for a simulated mediation participant; capitalize it when referring to the domain role.
- Use **Scenario** for the authored facts, constraints, resources, and conditions of an exercise.
- Use **Session** for one run of a Scenario.
- Use **Evaluator** for the planned post-Session review system. Never call it a judge, grader, or scoring engine.
- Use **rehearsal** for the product experience. Use “simulation” when explaining the mechanics.
- Use **community application** for the AGPL product and **managed service** for the future hosted offering.
- Do not call people “users” when a more specific role is known.

## 05. Writing style rules

### Sentence length

Lead with short, declarative sentences. Follow with medium-length explanation when the distinction matters. Use longer sentences only to preserve a necessary relationship between facts.

### Punctuation

- Prefer periods, commas, colons, and occasional semicolons.
- Avoid em dashes in public copy. Rewrite the sentence or use a colon.
- Do not use ellipses for suspense.
- Use exclamation marks rarely and never in warnings, evaluation, or support messages.
- Use rhetorical questions sparingly; answer them immediately when used.

### Capitalization

- Use sentence case for headings, buttons, and interface labels.
- Capitalize domain roles and objects when referring to their defined product meaning: Party, Mediator, Scenario, Session, Event, Projection, Offer, Evaluator.
- Do not capitalize generic mediation terminology merely for importance.

### Numbers

Use numerals for product facts, limits, dates, time, and ordered steps. Spell out numbers in ordinary prose when the number is not operationally important.

### Contractions

Use common contractions in conversational copy and support. Avoid them in legal terms, safety notices, and precise technical documentation.

### Active voice

Prefer active voice and name the responsible actor. Use passive voice only when the action matters more than the actor or the actor is genuinely unknown.

### Humor

Allow dry, observational humor about generic software and AI conventions. Never joke about Parties, conflict, confidentiality, protected information, poor performance, or someone asking for help.

### Evidence

Pair important claims with a visible mechanism or limitation. If a capability is planned, say so in the same sentence or section.

## 06. Channel adaptations

| Channel | Tone adjustment | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Most distilled and evocative; proof follows quickly | “Rehearse what cannot be scripted.” |
| Product interface | Brief, direct, and orienting | “Party A cannot observe this Caucus.” |
| Safety and privacy | Unambiguous, sober, and specific | “Do not enter real case files or confidential client information.” |
| Documentation | Technical enough to be reproducible; define terms once | “Each Event declares an audience before it enters a Party Projection.” |
| GitHub | Candid, precise, and collaborative | “The current fixtures are synthetic and intentionally small.” |
| Alpha outreach | Personal, respectful, and low-pressure | “We are inviting a small group of mediators to test where the rehearsal holds up and where it does not.” |
| Support | Calm ownership followed by a useful next step | “The Session did not save. Your provider key was not stored. Here is how to retry.” |
| Evaluator, future | Observational, evidence-linked, and non-prescriptive | “The Mediator moved to Caucus after Event 18. Review the preceding exchange and consider what changed.” |
| Community, future | Constructive, attributable, and specific | “Describe what the Scenario helped you practice and where its assumptions need revision.” |

## 07. Before and after

### Product introduction

**Before:**

> Unlock next-generation mediation mastery with our revolutionary AI platform.

**After:**

> Rehearse difficult mediations with independent simulated Parties.

**Why it works:** It names the action, audience, and product difference without hype.

### Confidentiality boundary

**Before:**

> Your data is completely safe and secure.

**After:**

> The closed alpha uses synthetic material. Do not submit real case files, privileged material, or confidential client information.

**Why it works:** It gives the practitioner a concrete boundary instead of an unverifiable promise.

### Party independence

**Before:**

> Experience realistic multi-agent AI conversations.

**After:**

> Each Party holds its own private facts, limits, and view of the Session.

**Why it works:** It explains what independence means inside a mediation.

### Evaluation

**Before:**

> Get objective AI scores that reveal the right mediation strategy.

**After:**

> Planned Session review will connect its observations to the Event log and professional standards. The mediator decides what to take from it.

**Why it works:** It preserves practitioner agency and labels future capability honestly.

### Error message

**Before:**

> Oops! Something went wrong. Please try again later!

**After:**

> Party B did not respond. Check its provider connection, then retry the turn.

**Why it works:** It identifies the failure and gives the next useful action.

## 08. Things we never say

- Hollow superlatives such as “world-class,” “best-in-class,” or “revolutionary.”
- Claims that Obversa predicts Parties, settlements, or real-world outcomes.
- Claims of objective, unbiased, or authoritative evaluation.
- Claims that redaction, self-hosting, or encryption makes material automatically safe.
- “AI will replace,” “AI knows,” or language that gives the model professional authority.
- False urgency, countdowns, scarcity theater, or pressure to join.
- Praise before substance: “Great question,” “Amazing work,” or “We love that for you.”
- Empty apologies. State what happened, take responsibility where appropriate, and give the next step.
- Faux-profound fragments that require a paragraph to explain them.
- Stacked abstractions such as “transformative human-centered intelligence.”
- More than one metaphor in a passage.
- Jokes about conflict, confidentiality, evaluation, or practitioner performance.

## 09. Voice check

Before publishing, ask:

1. Does this sound steady enough for someone preparing for a difficult Session?
2. Is the product fact more prominent than the AI terminology?
3. Does the language preserve the mediator's judgment?
4. Have we named the relevant boundary or limitation?
5. Could a generic coaching platform publish this unchanged?
6. Can any sentence be shortened without losing necessary meaning?
7. Is the copy useful, or merely atmospheric?
