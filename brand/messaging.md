# Obversa Messaging Framework

**Status:** Working messaging for closed alpha  
**Date:** 2026-08-30

## 01. Category descriptor

**Primary:** Mediation rehearsal  
**Expanded:** Professional simulation for mediators

Use “AI-assisted” as a factual product detail, not as the category or headline.

## 02. Core message

Obversa helps mediators rehearse difficult Sessions by placing them between independent simulated Parties with their own facts, limits, behavior, and view of the room.

## 03. Value proposition

Obversa gives practicing mediators and trainers a repeatable place to rehearse without coordinating human role players. Unlike generic AI role-play, each Party maintains separate private information, negotiation constraints, behavioral state, and Session visibility inside an inspectable mediation engine.

## 04. Tagline slate

## 05. Messaging hierarchy

### Level 1: Brand headline

**Practice the difficult mediation before it begins.**

### Level 2: Supporting statement

Work with independent simulated Parties that hold their own facts, limits, and view of the Session.

### Level 3: Key messages

#### Two Parties. Two private realities.

Each Party can use its own provider, model, persona, private facts, constraints, and behavioral profile. They do not share one undifferentiated chatbot context.

**Proof:** Exact-audience Projections give each Party only the Events it is authorized to observe.

#### Practice the process, not a script.

Obversa models Joint Session, private Caucus, Offers, Agreement, impasse, and Walkout. The practitioner decides how to respond; the product does not prescribe one winning line.

**Proof:** Deterministic domain rules control disclosure, negotiation state, thresholds, and terminal outcomes.

#### Your judgment stays in the chair.

Obversa is a rehearsal environment, not a mediator, judge, predictor, or substitute for professional judgment.

**Proof:** The human occupies the Mediator seat while simulated Parties respond within authored Scenario constraints.

#### Boundaries you can inspect.

The product makes Party separation, Event audiences, Scenario rules, and provider behavior explicit rather than hiding them behind “safe AI” language.

**Proof:** The community application is self-hostable and licensed under AGPL-3.0-only.

### Level 4: Current proof bank

1. Three versioned synthetic Scenarios are included in the current MVP.
2. Party A and Party B can use different OpenAI-compatible providers and models.
3. Each Party receives authored private context and an exact-audience Event Projection.
4. Joint Session and private Caucus are distinct Session phases.
5. Structured Offers, Agreement, impasse, and system-triggered Walkout are modeled explicitly.
6. Provider credentials remain in short-lived server memory and are excluded from Session storage and export.
7. Sanitized diagnostics expose latency, retries, token use, errors, and provider behavior without exposing credentials.
8. The code is open source under AGPL-3.0-only and supports local/self-hosted use.

## 07. Future platform message

Use this only in roadmap, partner, and long-form contexts. Do not place future features in the main closed-alpha value proposition.

**Working horizon statement:**

Obversa is building a practitioner learning loop: prepare source material, research and author credible Scenarios, rehearse, receive evidence-backed review, and improve through community knowledge.

### Future capability language

- **Evaluator:** “Planned evidence-backed Session review,” not “objective scoring” or “AI grades your mediation.”
- **Case research:** “Research-informed synthetic Scenarios,” not “real-case prediction” or “trained on confidential cases.”
- **Document preparation:** “Planned local redaction and preparation workflow,” not “documents are made safe automatically.”
- **Scenario ecosystem:** “Private libraries and curated public publishing,” not “an open marketplace” until contribution terms, review, and moderation exist.
- **Community:** “Practitioner feedback, reviews, and curation,” not “crowdsourced truth” or unqualified quality scores.

## 08. Audience-specific messaging

### Practicing mediators

- **They care about:** realistic preparation, autonomy, procedural integrity, confidentiality, and time.
- **Lead with:** repeatable private rehearsal before a difficult Session.
- **Strongest proof:** independent Party context, Caucus boundaries, exact-audience Projections, and no single prescribed response.
- **Avoid:** gamification, automated judgment, confidence scores, and claims that the simulation predicts real people.

### Mediation trainers and educators

- **They care about:** repeatable exercises, observable process choices, Scenario quality, and learner reflection.
- **Lead with:** consistent rehearsal that can complement human role-play and support later evidence-backed review.
- **Strongest proof:** versioned Scenarios, explicit process phases, Event-log review, and the future Scenario authoring system.
- **Avoid:** replacing instructors, automated certification, or claims of validated learning outcomes before research exists.

### Open-source practitioners and self-hosters

- **They care about:** inspectability, model choice, local control, extensibility, and honest boundaries.
- **Lead with:** an AGPL-licensed mediation engine with explicit Party separation and provider-agnostic runtimes.
- **Strongest proof:** source availability, self-hosting, OpenAI-compatible providers, deterministic domain rules, and synthetic fixtures.
- **Avoid:** implying that self-hosting alone solves privacy, security, retention, or compliance.

### Future small teams

- **They care about:** shared private Scenarios, predictable operations, deletion and retention controls, and support.
- **Lead with:** managed rehearsal infrastructure without requiring practitioners to manage provider keys.
- **Avoid:** enterprise claims about SSO, compliance, outcomes, or scale until those capabilities exist.

## 10. Things not to say

### Banned category clichés

- AI-powered communication coach
- Risk-free or completely safe practice
- Real-world results at scale
- Objective AI feedback
- Master difficult conversations
- Build confidence instantly
- Level up your mediation

### Unsupported claims

- Predicts how real Parties will behave
- Guarantees better settlements or outcomes
- Makes confidential documents safe
- Provides legal advice or mediation services
- Produces unbiased or objective evaluation
- Meets enterprise privacy, security, or regulatory standards not yet verified

### Voice failures

- Legal-tech theater: gavels, justice scales, “lex,” “juris,” verdict language
- Combat language: win, beat, crush, dominate, battle-test
- Therapy language: healing, emotional wellness, safe space
- AI mysticism: intelligent counterpart, synthetic empathy, cognitive twin
- False modesty and filler: quietly powerful, thoughtfully crafted, built for the future

## 11. Copy test

Before publishing any Obversa copy, ask:

1. Is this true in the current product, or clearly labeled as planned?
2. Does it respect mediator judgment instead of claiming authority?
3. Does it explain the independent-Party difference in plain language?
4. Could a generic AI coaching product say the same thing?
5. Does it avoid implying confidentiality, safety, objectivity, or outcomes we cannot prove?
