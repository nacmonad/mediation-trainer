# Obversa visual identity brief

**Status:** Working direction for design development  
**Date:** 2026-08-30

## 01. Identity strategy

Obversa should look like a professional instrument for holding multiple perspectives in relation. The identity must express separation without hostility, structure without bureaucracy, and seriousness without borrowing the visual language of law firms. Its central visual idea is the space between two distinct faces: not forced symmetry, not consensus, but a deliberate room in which both can be observed. The system should feel tactile and editorial around the brand, then become quieter and more operational inside the product.

## 02. Logo direction

### Working selection: Third Lens (study S)

Scott selected study S, **Third Lens**, as the tentative working mark on 2026-08-30. Its two continuous Party forms carve a lens-like third presence from negative space. Use it for current product and landing-page applications while retaining the full study archive; the selection is directional, not final approval.

### Primary direction

Use a **symbol and custom wordmark** system. The symbol should be able to stand alone as an application icon, favicon, Scenario attribution mark, and community publisher badge. The wordmark should be a restrained lowercase or title-case construction rather than an expressive display logo.

### Symbol concept: the obverse room

Construct the symbol from two opposing, non-touching forms held around a narrow vertical interval. Together they should suggest several ideas without literally illustrating any one of them:

- two faces or perspectives;
- the obverse and reverse of one object;
- two private rooms around a shared process;
- an abstract **O** whose center remains open;
- the Mediator's position between Parties.

The negative interval is essential. It represents process, attention, and the boundary that keeps the two sides distinct. The forms may be subtly unequal so the mark does not imply that mediation requires identical positions or perfect balance.

### Character

Composed, intelligent, dimensional, and quietly memorable. The mark should reward a second look without requiring an origin-story paragraph to make sense.

### Construction

- Begin with geometric arcs or softened slabs, then introduce small optical corrections by hand.
- Use a moderate stroke or filled construction that survives at 16 pixels.
- Preserve a clear interval between the two forms in every size.
- Test in one color before developing two-tone applications.
- Create horizontal, stacked, symbol-only, and single-color lockups.

### Wordmark

Start from the primary sans typeface, then customize the **O**, **v**, and spacing. The opening in the initial O can echo the symbol's central interval. Avoid exaggerated ligatures or turning every letter into a concept.

### What to avoid

- justice scales, gavels, columns, shields, courthouses, or handshake imagery;
- chat bubbles, quotation marks, speech waves, or chatbot sparkles;
- brains, neural nodes, circuits, gradients that signal “AI,” or humanoid faces;
- literal yin-yang, Venn diagram, eye, coin, or mirrored-head symbols;
- perfectly balanced bilateral symmetry that implies equivalence or guaranteed consensus;
- thin-line marks that collapse at favicon size;
- a decorative monogram made only from an O and V without the relational idea.

### Reference qualities

1. **Aesop:** Borrow the confidence of a typographic identity that does not perform innovation. Do not borrow its apothecary nostalgia.
2. **Muuto:** Borrow the humane geometry and calm use of space. Avoid becoming soft, domestic, or lifestyle-oriented.
3. **The New York Times Cooking:** Borrow the ability to pair editorial typography with a clear, useful digital product. Do not borrow its illustration language or consumer warmth wholesale.

These are quality references, not visual templates.

## 03. Color palette

### Core palette

| Token | Name | Hex | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ink` | Near black-green | `#17211F` | Primary text, dark surfaces, single-color mark |
| `paper` | Warm mineral | `#F2EFE7` | Primary brand background |
| `chalk` | Quiet white | `#FBFAF6` | Panels, cards, breathing space |
| `verdigris` | Deep verdigris | `#17685E` | Primary action, focus, active states |
| `copper` | Muted copper | `#B86243` | Human accent, highlights, selected editorial details |
| `slate` | Mineral grey | `#66736F` | Secondary text, metadata |
| `rule` | Soft boundary | `#D5D8D0` | Dividers, inactive controls, diagrams |

### Functional extensions

- Success: `#2F7458`
- Warning: `#A46822`
- Danger: `#A7423D`
- Dark surface: `#111916`
- Dark text: `#F2F3ED`

Functional colors communicate system state. They should never be reassigned to Party identities.

### Party color rule

Do not encode Party A as “good/cool” and Party B as “bad/warm.” Use equal-chroma variants derived from verdigris and copper only when the interface also provides explicit text labels, position, and icon shape. Private visibility must never depend on color alone.

### Personality and structure

This is a split-complementary, mineral palette: dark green-black for authority, verdigris for disciplined action, copper for human tension, and warm paper for tactility. It should feel closer to an annotated casebook and a well-made instrument than a financial dashboard.

### Usage

- Let paper, chalk, and ink occupy roughly 80 percent of brand compositions.
- Use verdigris for actions and process state.
- Use copper selectively for emphasis, never as a competing primary button color.
- The core mark must work in ink on paper and paper on ink.
- Avoid large teal-to-blue gradients, pure white expanses, and black-on-neon combinations.
- Verify WCAG contrast for every interface pairing; palette values are design starting points, not permission to skip testing.

## 04. Typography

### Editorial face: Source Serif 4

Use **Source Serif 4** for brand headlines, selected Scenario titles, pull quotations, and long-form editorial material. It is open source, highly legible, and serious without resembling a law-office default. Its optical sizes allow the same family to feel expressive at display scale and disciplined in reading contexts.

### Interface face: Geist Sans

Keep **Geist Sans** for navigation, product controls, body copy, tables, labels, and technical documentation. It is compact, clear, and already implemented in the application. It should carry most of the product so the interface remains operational rather than literary.

### Technical face: Geist Mono

Use **Geist Mono** for Event identifiers, timestamps, provider diagnostics, version numbers, and compact procedural metadata. Do not use monospaced type as a decorative signal of technical credibility.

### Hierarchy

- **Hero and editorial headlines:** Source Serif 4, 500 weight, tight but not compressed tracking.
- **Product page titles:** Geist Sans, 600 weight; Source Serif 4 is optional where the page is reflective rather than operational.
- **Subheads:** Geist Sans, 600 weight, sentence case.
- **Body:** Geist Sans, 400 to 450 weight, generous line height.
- **Labels and buttons:** Geist Sans, 600 weight, sentence case.
- **Metadata:** Geist Mono, 400 to 500 weight, never all caps by default.

### Avoid

- high-contrast fashion serifs;
- legal-document defaults such as Times New Roman;
- geometric sans faces with circular, friendly startup character everywhere;
- condensed display faces, rounded tech fonts, or monospaced body copy;
- all-cap navigation and excessive letter spacing.

## 05. Imagery and photography

### Overall aesthetic

Use restrained documentary photography and close observation rather than staged conflict. Images should feel present in real working environments: natural light, quiet concentration, material detail, and space around the subject.

### Subject matter

- hands annotating a Scenario or arranging materials;
- the geometry of tables, doors, thresholds, dividers, and adjacent rooms;
- practitioners preparing alone or reviewing after a Session;
- two occupied positions with a meaningful interval between them;
- marked-up documents made from visibly synthetic content;
- product details that reveal process boundaries and Event visibility.

Use real practitioners with consent when possible. Until then, prefer product imagery, objects, and environments over generic stock portraits.

### Treatment

- Natural or slightly warm light.
- Moderate contrast and restrained saturation.
- Paper, wood, mineral, and oxidized-metal tones may echo the palette.
- Crops may use architectural intervals or place subjects slightly off center.
- Preserve skin tones; do not wash photography in a brand-color overlay.

### Avoid

- adversarial people arguing across a table;
- smiling businesspeople shaking hands after a “win”;
- gavels, courtrooms, scales, legal pads, or staged lawyer imagery;
- glowing screens, holograms, robots, neural networks, or AI faces;
- empty diversity tableaux assembled as stock-photo proof;
- blurred blue offices and generic corporate meeting rooms.

## 06. Iconography and illustration

### Icons

Use regular-weight, open-form line icons built on a consistent 20 or 24 pixel grid. Ends may be gently squared rather than fully round. Icons are functional first: visibility, phase, Party, resource, Offer, review, warning, and provider state.

Every important state must combine an icon with text, position, or shape. A lock alone does not adequately explain an Event audience.

### Diagrams

Use planar diagrams with explicit lanes, intervals, and audience boundaries. Party perspectives may sit on opposing edges while the Mediator and shared process occupy the center. Favor rules, brackets, fields, and annotations over arrows that imply a simplistic linear funnel.

### Illustration

When a branded illustration is useful, build it from cropped planes, paired forms, and negative space derived from the symbol. Use flat color, subtle paper grain, and no character mascots. Illustration should clarify a relationship, not decorate an empty section.

## 07. Design principles

### Keep perspectives distinct

Do not merge Parties into one visual stream for the sake of neatness. Layout, labeling, and hierarchy should make private, shared, and Mediator-only information immediately legible.

### Let the interval work

Whitespace is not emptiness; it is the room that allows relationships to be read. Use deliberate separation instead of putting every idea in a bordered card.

### Show the mechanism

Trust comes from visible rules, audiences, states, and sources. Prefer an intelligible process diagram, Event label, or limitation over a decorative assurance badge.

### Calm the instrument

The interface supports attention during difficult material. Limit motion, color competition, floating surfaces, and ornamental controls; reserve emphasis for changes that require action or judgment.

### Texture at the edge, precision at the center

Brand pages may use paper texture, editorial type, photography, and copper accents. The live Session should become quieter, sharper, and predominantly sans serif so the practitioner can work without visual theater.

## 08. Brand expressions

### obversa.org

Open with the symbol, approved tagline, and a direct mediation-rehearsal explanation. Use editorial typography and one strong relational visual rather than a grid of feature cards. Move quickly into proof: independent Party views, real process phases, and the synthetic-data boundary.

### Product application

Prioritize Session state, Event audience, and Party distinction. Retain the existing three-lane Event structure, but refine it with the new palette, type hierarchy, and interval-based visual language. Avoid turning the Session into a chat application.

### Scenario library

Treat Scenarios like considered professional resources, not entertainment tiles. Lead with title, practice purpose, complexity, provenance, version, and review status. Ratings should never overpower written practitioner feedback or curation.

### Evaluator reports

Design future reports like annotated professional review: observations connected to Event evidence, standards, and reflective prompts. Do not use grades, leaderboards, celebratory confetti, radar charts that imply scientific precision, or red/green judgments without explanation.

### GitHub and documentation

Use the one-color mark, clear diagrams, restrained code samples, and explicit system boundaries. The open-source presentation should feel like the same brand, not a separate developer aesthetic.

### Presentations and research material

Use warm paper backgrounds, strong typographic hierarchy, narrow rules, and source notes. Keep one idea per slide and use copper only to direct attention. Research claims must remain visually connected to citations and limitations.

## 09. First design deliverables

1. Symbol exploration in black and white, with at least 12 materially different constructions around the “obverse room” idea.
2. Optical tests at 16, 24, 32, and 128 pixels.
3. Horizontal and stacked symbol-wordmark lockups.
4. Light, dark, and single-color applications.
5. A one-page obversa.org hero composition.
6. One Scenario library row and one live Session Event sequence using the system.
7. A future Evaluator excerpt to test whether the identity can support evidence-heavy material.

The identity is not approved until it works across the landing page, the operational product, and a dense review artifact.
