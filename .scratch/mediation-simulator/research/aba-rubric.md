## Summary

Two documents together define the canonical frame for grading mediator conduct:

1. **Model Standards of Conduct for Mediators (2005)** — jointly adopted by the ABA (Section of Dispute Resolution), the American Arbitration Association, and the Association for Conflict Resolution. Eight numbered standards (I–VIII) covering self-determination, impartiality, conflicts of interest, competence, confidentiality, quality of process, advertising, and fees. This is the natural backbone of an evaluator rubric because each standard is written as conduct obligations — i.e., gradeable behaviors.

2. **ABA Formal Opinion 518 (2025)** — ⚠ ABA Standing Committee on Ethics and Professional Responsibility opinion (per secondary summaries: issued August 2025) addressing a lawyer's ethical duties when serving as a mediator. It applies the lawyer Model Rules to the mediator role, with emphasis on confidentiality (Model Rule 1.6 posture in mediation, vs. the mediation privilege/confidentiality agreement), impartiality vs. candor tensions, dealing with unrepresented parties (Rule 4.3), and no communication with represented parties outside proper channels (Rule 4.2). For a transcript-based evaluator its practical value is an overlay on the Model Standards for lawyer-mediators; the eight standards remain the core grading categories.

For an LLM evaluator, the rubric skeleton below maps each standard to (a) a one-line definition and (b) observable behaviors detectable in a mediation transcript. Standards VII (advertising) and VIII (fees) are structurally invisible in a single-session transcript and should be excluded or reduced to a "n/a — not observable in transcript" outcome rather than graded as failures.

## Rubric skeleton

Grading scale suggestion per category: **observed / not observed / violation / not-observable-in-transcript**.

### I. Self-Determination
- **Definition**: The mediator supports each participant's free, informed decision-making and voluntary agreement; the parties, not the mediator, own the outcome.
- **Observable behaviors**:
  - Mediator presents options without prescribing or endorsing a specific settlement.
  - Parties articulate their own interests and proposals; mediator elicits rather than supplies them.
  - Mediator confirms parties understand terms before any agreement is recorded.
  - Mediator states (or the transcript shows) that parties may decline to agree or end the process.
  - ⚠ Red flag: mediator predicts/pressures a specific outcome ("you'll lose at trial, so take this").

### II. Impartiality
- **Definition**: The mediator must decline the appointment if unable to remain impartial and must avoid favoritism or bias in word, action, and appearance.
- **Observable behaviors**:
  - Balanced airtime and question distribution across parties.
  - Neutral, even-handed language; no disparagement of either party or side's position.
  - Consistent process rules applied to both parties (interruptions, caucuses, breaks).
  - ⚠ Red flags: verdict-like assessments of one party's merits, sympathetic alignment with one side, asymmetric coaching.

### III. Conflicts of Interest
- **Definition**: The mediator makes a reasonable inquiry into actual and potential conflicts, discloses them as soon as practicable, and proceeds only with informed consent or withdraws.
- **Observable behaviors**:
  - Opening disclosure/conflict-check statement at session start.
  - Mediator names prior relationships with parties/counsel when relevant.
  - ⚠ In a single transcript, mostly observable as a *check that disclosure happened*, not the underlying diligence — evaluator should grade the disclosure ritual and any unaddressed obvious conflict.

### IV. Competence
- **Definition**: The mediator accepts appointments only where qualified by training, experience, or other preparation, and maintains the skills the process requires.
- **Observable behaviors**:
  - Correct sequencing of the process (opening, information exchange, option generation, negotiation, agreement/closure).
  - Accurate, appropriate handling of procedural/legal-framework questions within the mediator's stated role (process guide, not legal advisor, unless disclosed otherwise).
  - Management of caucus logistics, agenda-setting, and reality-testing technique.

### V. Confidentiality
- **Definition**: The mediator keeps mediation information confidential consistent with the parties' agreement and applicable law, and explains the limits of confidentiality.
- **Observable behaviors**:
  - Explicit confidentiality explanation in the opening (including caucus confidentiality rules).
  - Mediator does not reveal caucus content to the other side without permission — the classic transcript-detectable violation.
  - Mediator clarifies what will/won't be shared before caucusing.
  - ⚠ From Opinion 518 overlay (unverified): lawyer-mediator must be clear that confidentiality governs both the lawyer's professional-conduct duty and the mediation's agreement/statutory privilege, and should explain any exceptions.

### VI. Quality of Process
- **Definition**: The mediator conducts the process diligently and honestly, does not coerce agreement, ensures participants understand the process, and may suspend or terminate a process that is being misused or is unproductive.
- **Observable behaviors**:
  - Sets and follows an agenda; keeps the discussion productive.
  - Addresses power/communication imbalances (interrupts bullying, gives the quieter party space).
  - Reality-tests proposals with neutral questions rather than pressure.
  - Ensures any agreement is reached voluntarily and recorded accurately (reads back terms, confirms assent).
  - Willing to call a recess or terminate rather than force a bad process.

### VII. Advertising / Solicitation of Employment
- **Definition**: The mediator is truthful in representing qualifications, services, and the mediation process.
- **Observable behaviors**: essentially none within a session transcript — mark **not-observable-in-transcript**; do not penalize.

### VIII. Fee Arrangements
- **Definition**: Fee arrangements should be reasonable and communicated in advance.
- **Observable behaviors**: at most a passing reference in the opening; mark **not-observable-in-transcript**; do not penalize.

### Opinion 518 overlay (lawyer-mediator) ⚠ unverified
- Confidentiality posture: lawyer-mediator must reconcile lawyer-client confidentiality duties with mediation confidentiality/privilege and explain the posture to participants.
- Unrepresented parties: heightened duty to avoid implying the lawyer-mediator is protecting an unrepresented party's interests (Rule 4.3 posture).
- Represented parties: no direct communication that circumvents counsel (Rule 4.2 posture), though mediation itself is typically consensual and agreed.
